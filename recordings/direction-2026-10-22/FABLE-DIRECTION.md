# FixList: CAC 2026 creative direction (final 3 days)

Written Thu Oct 22 2026, ~9:30 pm. Based on the repo, docs/video-shot-list.md, the 2026-10-18 promo and ad outputs, the overnight native evidence, and the 31 Simulator screenshots in recordings/demo-audit-2026-10-22/ (audit was still running; AUDIT.md not yet present).

## 0. Verdict in three lines

- Frame FixList as a **family tool**, not an elder tool: the person who opens it is the adult child after a parent's fall or hospital discharge. That fixes the "weak pull" Andrew himself identified, without changing a line of domain logic.
- The video leads with the **one-phone, three-people permission story** plus the **3D room scan and on-device "areas to review"**, because that is where judges see originality and coding depth. The checklist is the spine, not the headline.
- Build the **Vision room-recognition plugin** (half a day) and a **pre-seeded demo capture with AI flags** (half a day). Cut everything else that is not polish.

## 1. The framing

**Purpose sentence (say it verbatim, on screen and aloud):**
"FixList helps a family check an older relative's home for fall risks, agree on what to fix, and get the fixes done with people they trust."

**Audience (on screen):** Older adults living at home, the family members who worry about them, and the volunteers who help.

**Trigger moment:** "My grandmother fell last winter. The hospital handed us a paper checklist. Nobody knew who was fixing what." That is honest (Andrew should swap in a true version or say "a lot of families"), it explains why an elder would use it (someone they love set it up with them), and it reframes every "permission" feature as respect, not bureaucracy.

**Hook for the first 10 seconds:** open on the 3D Gaussian-splat room orbiting in the app with the caption "This is a room, scanned from a phone video." Then cut to the title card. Judges score creativity first; a splat rendered inside a React app built by a high-schooler is the single most surprising frame we own. Do not open on the language picker.

**What makes it original (say this, it is true):** three roles on one phone with permissions enforced in the domain layer; a "Yes" is never a plan item until the resident confirms; the AI only answers narrow photo questions and is labelled "may be wrong" everywhere; all data stays on the device.

## 3. Ranked in-app changes (about 1.5 days for one strong implementer)

1. **Vision room recognition plugin. Build it.** Verdict: yes, it is the best remaining hour-per-point investment. It gives Andrew the "camera CV + on-device AI" he wanted, it is honest by construction (it only picks a page), it runs in the Simulator, and it adds a Swift file judges can read. Scope: `FixListVision` Capacitor plugin with one method `classify(base64 jpeg) → [{label, confidence}]`; web fallback returns `unavailable`. Map the top room label (bathroom, bedroom, living_room, stairs, kitchen; hallway falls back to "not sure") to the room id; show a card "FixList recognized: Bathroom. On-device, may be wrong." with "Open the bathroom questions" and "Pick a different room". List up to three noticed objects (lamp, cord, bath) as plain words with no judgement. Files: new ios/App/App/FixListVisionPlugin.swift, src/lib/native-vision.ts, a small section in src/app/CaptureScreens.tsx or a new "Start from a photo" entry on the Check screen, i18n keys in en.ts and es.ts, one test for the label mapping. Risk: Simulator Vision classification works on the iOS 26.5 Simulator but confirm on day one; if it fails there, fall back to running it only when a photo is picked from the library and record on device only if time allows. Keep the threshold low and always show the "pick a different room" path so a wrong label never blocks.
2. **Pre-seeded demo capture with AI flags.** Without this the strongest honest tech (ARKit tour → sketch → areas to review) is invisible in a Simulator-only video. Scope: a "Load sample walk-through" button on the bathroom and living-room capture page, dev/demo-gated the same way as "Load local demo scan", that writes a capture record (3 to 5 photos, a closed outline, one door) and a matching flags file into IndexedDB. Photos must be license-clean: use the Openverse CC0/CC BY images already in private-captures/test-datasets/hazard-eval (check OPENVERSE.md rows) or frames from the Viennese video; never the playroom. Flags must be real model output run through scan tooling on those photos, not hand-typed scores. Files: src/lib/capture-storage.ts (seed helper), src/app/CaptureScreens.tsx, scripts/ios-demo-scan.mjs (bundle the sample into the ignored iOS assets), a validation test. Risk: the capture hash binding is strict (good); generate the flags file from the exact bundled bytes.
3. **Hide the demo banner after the first screen.** "Demo profiles on this device — no real accounts or sign-in yet" currently sits above every screen and eats a fifth of the viewport in video. Show it on the language and person-picker screens, and in About and the report, and drop it elsewhere; the "Fictional Maple House" naming already carries the honesty. Files: src/app/PhoneShell.tsx or App.tsx, display tests. Risk: low; keep a test that it still appears on start/people/about.

Drop the word "sample" from question text (keep it in room names). Files: src/i18n/en.ts, es.ts, catalog tests. Risk: Spanish strings must be regenerated; snapshot tests will need updating.

## 4. Written-answer angles (one line each so the implementer does not have to invent them)

- Technical difficulty: hash-bound AI flags. Flags only load if the sha256 of every photo matches the capture on the phone, so a stale or wrong file can never attach to the wrong room; also the permission model in the domain layer so a volunteer view cannot read a resident's unshared answers even by URL.
- What Andrew learned: that "AI helps" is a design decision, not a model: the app only asks the model narrow photo questions and shows the question it answered.
- 2.0: real accounts and server-checked permissions, a partner volunteer organization, reviewed Spanish, Foundation Models on-device when iOS 27 SDK ships, and a validated hazard study before any score is called more than experimental.

## 5. Top risks and how to avoid them

1. **Video overruns 3:00 or misses a required element.** Mitigation: the storyboard puts name + purpose + audience at 0:08 and tools at 2:26; render with scripts/promo/build-video.mjs and run verify-video.mjs for duration; have one person check the rules list against the final cut before upload.
2. **The Vision plugin does not classify in the Simulator.** Mitigation: spike it first thing Friday morning in a throwaway Xcode project; if it fails, record scene 5 on Andrew's physical iPhone 17 with the existing build (works without the iOS 27 SDK for Vision) or drop scene 5 and extend scene 6.
3. **Seeded flags look fabricated.** Mitigation: generate them with scan/suggest.py from the actual bundled photos and keep the model name and threshold on screen; never edit scores by hand; mention "sample walk-through" in the UI label.
4. **AI-use disclosure is incomplete.** Mitigation: the credits card and docs/ai-disclosures.md must list Claude and Codex model families used for code, the StartLux weights (CC BY-NC) and the Vision framework. Andrew writes his own one paragraph on what he personally built, understood and decided; the implementer must not write that for him.
5. **Honesty slips in narration.** Banned words in the script: detect, find hazards, safe, assessment, vetted, verified. Allowed: areas to review, may be wrong, Margaret decides, reported by people.
6. **License trap in footage.** Only the Viennese CC BY-SA scan and Openverse CC0/CC BY photos on screen; the credits card carries the Viennese attribution exactly as docs/video-shot-list.md spells it.
7. **Last-minute regressions.** Freeze features Saturday noon; Saturday afternoon and Sunday are recording, cutting, and the written answers. Run `npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm run ios:demo` before every recording session and keep the Simulator at iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26.5, Do Not Disturb on, 9:41 status bar.
